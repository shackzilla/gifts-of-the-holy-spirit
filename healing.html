GIFTS OF THE HOLY SPIRIT · BIBLE STUDY

The Gift of Healing

A Bible study on prayer, compassion, and God’s healing work

STUDY FOUR • HEALING

“To another gifts of healing by the one Spirit.” — 1 Corinthians 12:9

Paul names “gifts of healing” among the Spirit’s manifestations for the common good. This study considers what the passage says, how the church is instructed to pray for the sick, and how to care faithfully for people whether healing is immediate, gradual, or not seen in the way requested. Christians differ on the operation of this gift today; the study distinguishes biblical teaching from proposed explanations and personal experiences.

1 What Are the Gifts of Healing

In 1 Corinthians 12:9 Paul uses plural language often rendered “gifts of healing” or “gifts of healings.” He repeats the phrase in verses 28 and 30, but does not explain why it is plural, assign categories of illness, or provide a method for exercising it. Interpretations of the plural differ. We should not build a detailed system from a grammatical feature that Paul does not explain.

The immediate context supplies the clearest purpose: the Spirit gives manifestations for the common good, and the members of Christ’s body need one another. Paul’s question in verse 30 expects that not everyone has the same gift. At the same time, James 5 calls the church to pray for the sick; prayer is not limited to people identified as having a healing gift.

2 Healing in the Ministry of Jesus

The Gospels portray Jesus healing people with compassion and authority to forgive sin. His healings are also signs that reveal who He is and what God’s kingdom is bringing. We should read those accounts as testimony to Jesus, not as a formula that every believer can repeat at will. The healer in the Gospel narrative is the Lord to whom the church prays.

Jesus often responds to particular people rather than treating them as examples for an audience. In Mark 2:1–12, the healing of the paralytic is linked to Jesus’ authority to forgive sins. That narrative does not license us to presume that a sick person’s illness was caused by a particular sin. (RE: John 9:1-3) Personal blame requires evidence and should never be invented from a condition.

3 Prayer for the Sick

James 5:13–16 tells a sick person to call the elders of the church, who pray and anoint in the name of the Lord. The passage encourages expectant prayer and acknowledges the Lord as the one who raises up. It also includes confession and mutual prayer, but it does not teach that every illness is the result of personal sin.

Figure 2 Prayer continues in practical care

Prayer can be offered plainly and without performance. Ask what the person would welcome, respect their privacy, and avoid public details unless they consent. Continue companionship, meals, transport, or other appropriate help after the prayer ends.

4 Healing and Unanswered Requests

Scripture includes remarkable healings and also records believers who remained ill or faced ongoing affliction. Paul describes Epaphroditus as seriously ill before God showed mercy (Philippians 2:25–30), and he says he left Trophimus sick in Miletus (2 Timothy 4:20). In 2 Corinthians 12:7–10, Paul’s repeated plea about his “thorn” receives an answer concerning grace and strength in weakness; the precise nature of the thorn is disputed.

These passages caution against promising a particular timetable or treating an unhealed person as a failure. We may pray earnestly for healing and still acknowledge that we do not control the outcome. A delayed or different answer does not remove a person’s worth, God’s compassion, or the church’s duty to care.

Careful statementWhy it matters
“We will pray for healing.”Expresses a genuine request to God.
“We will remain with you.”Commits the church to care beyond a single moment.
“I cannot promise an outcome.”Speaks truthfully without weakening compassion.
“Your illness is not proof of weak faith.”Rejects an unsupported and harmful judgment.

5 Healing and Ordinary Care

Prayer and appropriate medical care can be pursued together. Receiving care does not show a lack of faith, and prayer should not be used to pressure someone to stop treatment or ignore urgent symptoms. People facing illness deserve truthful information, personal agency, and support from those responsible for their care.

Spiritual support can include listening, asking permission before touching or praying aloud, protecting confidential details, and helping with practical needs. When someone reports improvement, celebrate with gratitude while remaining accurate about what is known. Do not declare a medical condition resolved on the basis of a momentary impression.

6 Test Claims and Protect People

A testimony about healing can encourage others, but it should be told accurately and with the person’s permission. Distinguish a prayer request, an observed change, and a verified medical conclusion. Do not use someone’s suffering to establish the status of a speaker, solicit money, or create pressure for a public display.

Figure 3 A responsible way to assess a healing testimony

When no healing is apparent, do not invent an explanation or blame the person. Continue prayer if welcomed, offer practical support, and make room for grief or disappointment. The church serves the sick person, not a preferred account of the event.

7 A Practical Prayer and Care Journal

These prompts help a person or small group pray responsibly and follow through. Record only information that the affected person has agreed may be shared.

PromptRecord
What has the person requested?Use their words and respect their boundaries.
Who may be involved?Identify elders, family, and care partners with permission.

Reflection and Discussion

  • How does 1 Corinthians 12 place healing within the common good?
  • What does James 5 instruct the church to do when someone is sick?
  • How can we pray expectantly without promising an outcome God has not disclosed?
  • What practical care can our group offer after prayer?

A Prayer for Healing and Compassion

Father, we bring those who are sick before You and ask for healing in the name of Jesus. Give wisdom to those caring for them, relief where there is pain, and strength for each day. Teach us to pray with faith and speak with honesty. Keep us from blaming those who suffer. Help our church remain present in love, whatever the outcome, and let every good gift bring gratitude to You. Amen.

Further Study

Read 1 Corinthians 12–14 as a whole. Then read James 5:13–16, Mark 2:1–12, Acts 3:1–16, Philippians 2:25–30, and 2 Corinthians 12:7–10 in context. Note what each passage explicitly teaches and which questions it leaves open.
