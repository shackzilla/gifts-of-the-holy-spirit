GIFTS OF THE HOLY SPIRIT · BIBLE STUDY

Preface

The gifts of the Holy Spirit have been a source of encouragement, discussion, and disagreement throughout the Christian church. Scripture teaches that these gifts are given by the Spirit for the common good and for the building up of the body of Christ.

This study guide is designed to examine those gifts carefully through the lens of Scripture. Its purpose is not to rank gifts, elevate spiritual experiences, or determine who is more spiritually gifted. Instead, it asks practical questions: What does Scripture say? What is the purpose of each gift? How should it be exercised? And how should spiritual claims be tested?

Throughout this study, we will distinguish between what Scripture clearly teaches, how passages are interpreted, and what people may experience or claim today. Because Christians hold different views about some spiritual gifts, this study seeks to approach those differences with honesty, humility, and respect.

Above all, spiritual gifts are about serving others. They should be exercised in love, humility, accountability, and a desire to strengthen the church and bring glory to Christ.

May this study encourage a deeper understanding of God's Word, a greater dependence upon the Holy Spirit, and a willingness to faithfully use whatever gifts God has given for the good of others.

Let us study with open hearts, discerning minds, humble spirits, and our eyes fixed on Christ.

Course Introduction

The Holy Spirit gives gifts to serve others and build up the church. This course examines the gifts named in Scripture, beginning with the message of wisdom. Each study asks what the biblical passage says, what purpose the gift serves, how it may be exercised with love and humility, and how a church can responsibly evaluate what is offered.

The aim is not to identify who has the most gifts or to make unusual experiences the center of Christian life. Paul writes that the Spirit’s manifestations are given “for the common good” (1 Corinthians 12:7). A gift is therefore best understood in relation to the people it serves. Its use should direct attention to Christ, strengthen the church, and reflect the character the Spirit forms in believers.

Biblical Foundations for the Course

The New Testament discusses gifts in several places, including 1 Corinthians 12–14, Romans 12:3–8, Ephesians 4:11–16, and 1 Peter 4:10–11. These passages have different emphases. First Corinthians addresses the diversity of gifts within one body and the need for love and order. Romans connects gifts with sober judgment and faithful service. Ephesians describes gifts in relation to equipping believers and growing toward maturity. First Peter urges believers to use what they have received as stewards who serve one another.

The passages should be read in their own contexts. They are not presented as a single ranking of Christians or a complete checklist that every person must satisfy. The Spirit distributes gifts; believers are called to use them faithfully.

Christians hold different views about how some gifts operate today. This course will distinguish what a passage explicitly teaches from a proposed interpretation or modern example. Where Christians disagree, the studies will identify the question rather than claim a certainty the text does not provide.

Three Principles That Govern the Gifts

Although the gifts of the Holy Spirit differ in their expression and purpose, Scripture gives several principles that help the church understand how they should function. Throughout this study, three principles will appear repeatedly: the common good, discernment, and testing. These principles do not replace love, humility, accountability, or faithfulness to Scripture. Rather, they provide a framework through which those qualities are put into practice.

How the Three Work Together

These three principles provide a simple pattern that can be carried through every study in this course:

COMMON GOOD → DISCERNMENT → TESTING

Common Good: Who does this serve, and does it build up the Body of Christ?

Discernment: What is actually being presented, and how should it be understood?

Testing: Does it withstand examination by Scripture, truth, character, fruit, and the believing community?

Together they help keep spiritual gifts in their proper biblical setting. The common good gives the gifts their direction, discernment encourages careful understanding, and testing provides accountability. Love and humility should govern all three, while Scripture remains the authoritative standard by which every spiritual claim is ultimately measured.

The Shared Posture

“To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good.” — 1 Corinthians 12:7

The gifts belong to the Spirit. They are received, not earned as a badge of spiritual standing. No believer possesses every gift, and no single gift makes its recipient more valuable than another member of the body. Paul’s picture of the church in 1 Corinthians 12 calls believers to depend on and care for one another.

Love governs the use of every gift. Paul places 1 Corinthians 13 between his discussions of gifts in chapters 12 and 14. Even a striking ability or persuasive message cannot replace patience, kindness, truthfulness, and concern for others. In 1 Corinthians 14:26, he directs the gathered church toward building one another up.

For that reason, a claimed gift does not excuse arrogance, coercion, secrecy from appropriate counsel, or refusal to be corrected. A person may speak sincerely and still misunderstand an impression or apply a passage poorly. Humility allows a believer to offer something for consideration, listen to questions, and acknowledge a mistake.

Gifts and Spiritual Maturity

Gifts and maturity are related, but they are not identical. A person may communicate a useful insight and still need to grow in character. Likewise, quiet acts of service may strengthen the church without attracting attention. The fruit of the Spirit described in Galatians 5:22–23 gives us an important picture of the character Christians are called to cultivate.

This course will therefore ask two questions throughout: What is being offered? and How is it being offered? Truth matters, and so can motive, manner, accountability, and the effect on the people involved. Gifts should be exercised with respect, humility, and consideration for the people they are intended to serve.

Testing and Receiving What Is Offered

Scripture instructs believers to test spiritual claims rather than accept or dismiss them solely because they sound impressive or feel powerful (1 Thessalonians 5:19–22; 1 John 4:1). Testing begins with the biblical text. It also involves careful attention to facts, the character of the claim, its consequences, and the counsel of mature believers.

Testing need not be hostile. It protects the church and the person offering the gift. A thoughtful response can say, “Let us consider this carefully,” without pretending to know more than is known. When a matter affects another person’s reputation, safety, or major decisions, patience and appropriate accountability are especially important.

How to Use Each Study

1. Read the cited passages in context. Notice who is speaking, who is being addressed, and the

concern the passage answers. 2. Separate text from interpretation. Identify what Scripture states directly before deciding how it

applies to a present situation. 3. Discuss significant questions with mature believers. Invite correction and listen to people who may

see something you have missed. 4. Work through the reflection prompts honestly. You do not need to force an answer where the

evidence is incomplete. 5. Choose an act of faithful service. The goal of study is not merely to define a gift, but to love God

and serve others more faithfully.

In Conclusion

The gifts of the Holy Spirit are given for the good of others and the strengthening of the church. As we study them, our goal is to understand Scripture faithfully, remain open to the Spirit’s work, and exercise every gift with love and humility. We can ask questions, test what is offered, and acknowledge what we do not yet know. With that posture, we begin with the gift of wisdom: learning to seek God’s truth and apply it in ways that serve others and honor Christ.
