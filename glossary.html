Glossary of Spiritual Gifts Terms

These definitions help readers use the course consistently. They are brief working descriptions rather than exhaustive doctrinal statements. Where Christians dispute a term, the entry names the disagreement. Scripture in context and the teaching of one’s church remain more important than a glossary label.

Biblical and Theological Terms

TermWorking definition and key text
Charisma / charismataGreek terms associated with a gracious gift or gifts. The New Testament uses them in more than one context; see Romans 12:6 and 1 Corinthians 12:4.
Manifestation of the SpiritPaul’s description of what the Spirit gives for the common good in 1 Corinthians 12:7.
Spiritual giftA grace given by the Spirit for service. Lists and functions vary across 1 Corinthians 12, Romans 12, Ephesians 4, and 1 Peter 4.
Fruit of the SpiritThe character described in Galatians 5:22–23; distinct from the varied gift lists.
Common goodThe benefit of others and the church, not a person’s spiritual status (1 Corinthians 12:7).
Edification / building upStrengthening the church in understanding, love, and maturity (1 Corinthians 14:26; Ephesians 4:12–16).

Terms Used in the Individual Studies

TermWorking definition and caution
Message of wisdomThe manifestation named in 1 Corinthians 12:8; Paul does not give a detailed technical definition.
Message of knowledgeNamed beside wisdom in 1 Corinthians 12:8; the exact boundary between the two is debated.
Gift of faithFaith named as a particular manifestation in 1 Corinthians 12:9, distinguished in discussion from the trust in Christ shared by believers.
Gifts of healingPaul’s plural expression in 1 Corinthians 12:9, 28, 30; the significance of the plural is not explained in the passage.
Working of miraclesNamed in 1 Corinthians 12:10; Paul does not prescribe a method for producing miracles.
ProphecySpirit related speech discussed in 1 Corinthians 12–14; Christians differ about its authority and continuation today.
Distinguishing between spiritsA manifestation named in 1 Corinthians 12:10; related to but distinct from the duty of believers to test claims (1 John 4:1).
TonguesSpeech named in 1 Corinthians 12:10 and described in Acts; its form across the passages is debated.
TermWorking definition and caution
Interpretation of tonguesMaking a public tongue intelligible to the gathered church (1 Corinthians 14:26–28).

Terms for Careful Discernment

TermMeaning in this course
ObservationWhat someone actually heard, saw, felt, or recorded before assigning a cause.
InterpretationThe proposed meaning of an observation or passage; it may need correction.
ClaimA statement offered to others as true, especially one asserting a spiritual source or required response.
Testing / weighingExamining a claim by Scripture, its content, facts, love, counsel, and fruit (1 Thessalonians 5:19–22; 1 Corinthians 14:29).
FruitA pattern of character and conduct over time; Matthew 7:15–20 addresses false prophets, and Galatians 5:22–23 describes the Spirit’s fruit.
AccountabilityThe speaker’s willingness to submit a claim and its use to responsible examination and correction.

Historical Positions and Language

TermWorking definition
CessationismThe view that certain foundational or revelatory gifts ceased as ordinary gifts after the apostolic era; proponents differ in details.
ContinuationismThe view that Scripture does not teach the cessation of all such gifts, while modern claims remain subject to testing.
GlossolaliaA later scholarly term for speaking in tongues.
XenolaliaSpeaking an identifiable human language one has not learned; a narrower term used in historical discussion.
ConfessionA church’s formal doctrinal statement, such as the Westminster Confession of Faith.
SessionIn Presbyterian church government, the body of elders responsible for the oversight of a local congregation.

How to Use This Glossary

A term can be useful without settling a debate. When discussing a claim, ask the speaker what they mean, read the cited passage in context, and distinguish a biblical description from a later theological label. Appendix C maps the passages; Appendix D supplies a testing framework; Appendix E explains major Presbyterian perspectives.
