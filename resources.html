Further Reading & Sources

The following references consolidate the Scripture passages, historical materials, theological documents, and scholarly sources recommended throughout Gifts of the Holy Spirit • Bible Study. Repeated passages have been combined so this section can serve as a single reference guide at the end of the study.

Scripture and Biblical References

[1] 1 Corinthians 12–14. The principal New Testament discussion of spiritual gifts, the common good, love, prophecy, tongues, interpretation, testing, and orderly worship.

[2] Romans 12:1–8. Spiritual gifts, transformed thinking, sober judgment, and differing forms of service within the body of Christ.

[3] Ephesians 4:1–16. Unity, spiritual gifts, equipping the saints, maturity, and growth into Christ.

[4] 1 Peter 4:7–11. Stewardship of gifts through speaking and serving, with the purpose of glorifying God.

[5] James 1:5–8. Asking God for wisdom and the relationship between faith and doubt.

[6] James 3:13–18. Wisdom from above and its qualities, including purity, peace, gentleness, mercy, good fruit, impartiality, and sincerity.

[7] Proverbs 2:1–6. Seeking wisdom, understanding, and the knowledge of God.

[8] Proverbs 9:10. The fear of the Lord as the beginning of wisdom.

[9] Acts 15:1–21. Communal deliberation involving testimony, Scripture, discernment, and practical consequences.

[10] 1 Corinthians 1:18–31. The contrast between God's wisdom and worldly measures of status and wisdom.

[11] 1 Corinthians 2:6–16. God's wisdom, the Spirit's work, and spiritual understanding.

[12] 1 Corinthians 8:1–3. Knowledge, pride, and the necessity of love.

[13] 1 Corinthians 13:1–13. Love, the limitations of knowledge and prophecy, and the proper perspective on spiritual gifts.

[14] Colossians 1:9–10. Knowledge of God's will and knowledge that produces a worthy and fruitful life.

[15] John 4:1–42, especially 4:16–19. Jesus' conversation with the Samaritan woman and His knowledge of her circumstances.

[16] Acts 5:1–11. Peter's confrontation of Ananias and Sapphira.

[17] 1 Corinthians 12:7–11, 27–30. The Spirit's distribution of gifts for the common good, including faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, discernment, tongues, and interpretation.

[18] Mark 9:14–29. Faith, prayer, dependence upon Jesus, and the father's admission of both belief and unbelief.

[19] Hebrews 11:1–40. Faith expressed through trust and obedience amid differing earthly outcomes.

[20] James 5:13–18. Prayer for those who suffer or are sick, including the involvement of church elders.

[21] 2 Corinthians 5:7. Walking by faith rather than sight.

[22] Romans 10:17. Faith and hearing the message of Christ.

[23] Mark 1:29–34. Jesus' healing ministry and compassion.

[24] Mark 2:1–12. The healing of the paralytic and Jesus' authority to forgive sins.

[25] Acts 3:1–16. The healing of the man unable to walk and Peter's redirection of attention toward Jesus.

[26] Acts 9:32–35. The healing of Aeneas.

[27] Philippians 2:25–30. Epaphroditus' serious illness and recovery.

[28] 2 Timothy 4:20. Trophimus being left sick at Miletus.

[29] 2 Corinthians 12:7–10. Paul's affliction, prayer, and God's sufficient grace.

[30] Acts 14:8–18. The healing at Lystra and Paul and Barnabas' refusal to receive worship.

[31] Acts 19:11–20. Extraordinary miracles through Paul and the misuse of the name of Jesus.

[32] Hebrews 2:1–4. Signs, wonders, miracles, and God's testimony concerning the message of salvation.

[33] 1 Corinthians 14:1–5. Prophecy, strengthening, encouragement, consolation, and building up the church.

[34] 1 Corinthians 14:26–33. Orderly worship, building up the church, and weighing what is spoken.

[35] 1 Thessalonians 5:19–22. Not despising prophecies, testing everything, and holding fast to what is good.

[36] Acts 11:27–30. Agabus' prophecy concerning a coming famine and the church's practical response.

[37] Acts 21:10–14. Agabus' warning concerning Paul's journey and the response of the believers.

[38] 1 John 4:1–6. Testing spiritual claims and examining teaching concerning Jesus Christ.

[39] Matthew 7:15–20. Jesus' warning concerning false prophets and the importance of examining their fruit.

[40] Acts 16:16–18. Paul's encounter with the slave girl described as having a spirit of divination.

[41] Acts 2:1–41. Pentecost, the coming of the Holy Spirit, and speaking in other tongues.

[42] Acts 10:44–48. The Holy Spirit coming upon Gentile believers and their speaking in tongues.

[43] Acts 19:1–7. The disciples at Ephesus receiving the Holy Spirit and speaking in tongues and prophesying.

[44] Galatians 5:13–26. Life by the Spirit, the works of the flesh, and the fruit of the Spirit.

[45] John 15:1–17. Abiding in Christ, bearing fruit, and remaining in His love.

[46] Romans 8:1–17. Life according to the Spirit and the believer's relationship to God through the Spirit.

[47] Colossians 3:12–17. Christian character, love, peace, thankfulness, and life together in Christ.

[48] Romans 12:6–8. Prophecy, service, teaching, exhortation, giving, leading, and mercy.

[49] 1 Corinthians 12:28. Apostles, prophets, teachers, miracles, gifts of healing, helping, administrating, and tongues.

[50] Ephesians 4:11–16. Apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, and teachers and their role in equipping the church.

[51] 1 Peter 4:10–11. Speaking and serving as stewardship of God's gifts.

Primary Passages for Course-Wide Review

[61] 1 Corinthians 12:7. The common good as the purpose of the Spirit's manifestation.

[62] 1 Corinthians 13:1–13. Love as essential to the proper exercise of spiritual gifts.

[63] 1 Corinthians 14:26. Building up the church as the governing purpose of gathered contributions.

[64] 1 Thessalonians 5:19–22; 1 John 4:1–6. The responsibility to test spiritual claims.

[65] Galatians 5:16–26. The Spirit's work in forming Christian character and the fruit of the Spirit.

[66] Ephesians 4:11–16. Unity, maturity, service, and growth in Christ.

Suggested Reading Order

[67] Begin with 1 Corinthians 12–14 as a complete unit.

[68] Read Romans 12:1–8, Ephesians 4:1–16, and 1 Peter 4:7–11 to compare the different New Testament discussions of spiritual gifts.

[69] Read Galatians 5:13–26 alongside 1 Corinthians 12–13 to consider the relationship between spiritual gifts, character, and love.

[70] For questions concerning prophecy and discernment, read 1 Corinthians 14:1–33, 1 Thessalonians 5:19–22, and 1 John 4:1–6 together.

[71] For questions concerning tongues and interpretation, read 1 Corinthians 12–14 alongside Acts 2:1–41, Acts 10:44–48, and Acts 19:1–7.

[72] For historical and denominational questions concerning tongues and spiritual gifts, consult the Assemblies of God, Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Westminster Confession of Faith, and Orthodox Presbyterian Church materials listed above.

[73] When comparing theological interpretations, distinguish the biblical text itself from later theological conclusions, denominational teaching, historical interpretation, and contemporary personal experience.
